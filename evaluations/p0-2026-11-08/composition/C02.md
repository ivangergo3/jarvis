# Acquisition experiment economics

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is the acquisition cost assigned to each new paying account. Here the measured cash CAC is **€100 per account**: €900 divided by 9 paying accounts under the same cost scope. This says what the experiment paid to acquire accounts; whether that cost is recovered depends on the gross profit those accounts generate over time.

These are hypothetical cohort figures. The acquisition dates and reporting dates are unspecified, so use this one cohort consistently and record its exact dates before comparing it with another experiment. The denominator is new paying accounts, not users, leads, or trials. The €900 includes the supplied acquisition cash spend and excludes unvalued founder time; other acquisition costs have not been established. Call it paid-channel CAC only if that spend and the attributed accounts are specifically from paid channels.

| Metric | Definition and calculation | Meaning and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Cohort cash CAC | €900 acquisition cash spend / 9 new paying accounts = **€100/account** | Valid under the supplied matching cost/attribution scope; not fully loaded CAC |
| Monthly revenue per account | **€40/account/month**, supplied | A monthly revenue rate, not proof each account earned €40 in its joining month |
| Monthly gross profit per account | €40 × 75% = **€30/account/month** | Revenue remaining after costs included in gross margin; not net profit |
| Implied monthly cost of revenue per account | €40 × 25% = **€10/account/month** | Based on the supplied margin; confirm which service costs it includes |
| Cohort monthly revenue run rate | 9 × €40 = **€360/month** | Assumes all nine remain paying at this rate for a full month |
| Cohort monthly gross-profit run rate | 9 × €30 = **€270/month** | Same full-month assumption; excludes acquisition cost and other expenses |
| Simple cash-CAC payback scenario | €100 / €30 per month = **3.33 full paid months/account** | Conditional estimate, not observed payback; assumes uninterrupted payment, constant price and margin, and no additional acquisition cost |

Accounts joined throughout the month. Consequently, neither €360 revenue nor €270 gross profit is established as the actual joining-month total. Calculate realized cohort revenue and cost of revenue from dated records, including proration, refunds, and cancellations. To measure acquisition-cost recovery, compare cumulative realized cohort gross profit with €900, tracking elapsed time from acquisition. Cash recovery additionally depends on collection and payment timing.

Founder sales time was 12 hours, or **1.33 hours per acquired account** on this cohort allocation. With no agreed hourly value, fully loaded CAC cannot be reported as a measured number. If an agreed value is `v` euros/hour, the cost including that time would be:

`(€900 + 12 × v) / 9 = €100 + 1.33 × v per account`

Other omitted acquisition costs would also need adding. For illustration only, valuing time at €50/hour adds €600 to cohort cost, yielding €166.67/account and a corresponding simple gross-profit payback scenario of 5.56 paid months. The €50 rate is an example, not a valuation of the founder's time or a result of this experiment.

Lifetime value (LTV) means value accumulated over an account's paying lifetime. Revenue LTV and gross-profit LTV answer different questions: the latter accounts for service costs and is the relevant basis for this payback comparison. Neither can be estimated credibly here because there is no retention history. A constant-churn formula would invent the missing lifetime assumption. Likewise, there is no supported LTV/CAC ratio or conclusion that the experiment is profitable.

The immediate business reading is: each €100 of measured acquisition cash needs approximately 3.33 full paid months of €30 monthly gross profit to be recovered under unchanged conditions. As a teaching example, an account that stays for only two full paid months contributes €60 gross profit, leaving €40 of that cash CAC unrecovered before other costs. Retention therefore determines whether the attractive-looking monthly revenue accumulates long enough to repay acquisition.

Next measurements should make that uncertainty observable:

1. Record acquisition source, attributed spend, first-paid date, and account identity, with a consistent attribution rule. Track trials and users separately from paying accounts.
2. Track realized revenue, refunds, cost of revenue, and collections by account and date. Confirm the 75% margin's cost inclusions and compute actual cumulative cohort gross profit and recovery time.
3. Follow accounts at comparable ages, such as completed paid months since joining. Customer retention at age `t` is original-cohort accounts still paying at that age divided by original-cohort accounts with enough follow-up to reach that age. Report the eligible count so younger accounts are not mistaken for retained mature accounts. Define customer churn over a specified interval separately from revenue retention, which captures changes in recurring revenue as well as losses.
4. Agree how to value founder time and include other acquisition costs before comparing fully loaded CAC across channels or cohorts.

This provides a baseline to evaluate as the cohort matures; it does not support a spending recommendation.
