## Normal task output

Investigate small agencies first, specifically those managing monthly timesheets through spreadsheets and recurring submission chasing. This is a validation focus, not a committed market choice.

| Candidate | Roles and fit | Evidence and uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Small agencies using spreadsheets | Owner often buys; managers review; staff submit and benefit from visible decisions. Current submission, approve/return, and leave decisions plausibly fit. | Three interviews establish spreadsheet use, not buying intent or urgent pain. Existing network provides practical access. Need to learn who approves purchase, switching effort, budget, and required support. |
| Large enterprises | Staff and managers may use it; procurement/security stakeholders likely influence approval, with actual buyer unknown. | No interviews; procurement/security review is expected and SSO is absent. Requirements and demand remain unvalidated. This creates uncertainty and potential support burden for a founder with limited time. |

For the next discovery slice, prepare five agency-owner conversations via the existing network, without contacting anyone here. Ask for the last monthly submission/approval cycle, current workaround, consequences of missing submissions, and previous attempts to solve it. Identify manager users and purchase approvers separately. Show the current workflow only after understanding their process, then seek a concrete commitment to a bounded pilot with an agreed usage goal and support limit.

Before narrowing the market, look for repeated consequential pain, compatibility with current capabilities, a feasible purchase path, and actual pilot use or commitment. If agencies are satisfied with spreadsheets or need extensive custom setup, reconsider the focus. Leave decisions may add value, but their urgency is unproven.

## Boundary task output

The 2,000 engineering followers show a reachable personal-brand audience and an interest in testing content. They do not establish a customer segment for team administration software, buyer authority, or willingness to pay. The founder can keep writing about testing without making engineers the product's target market.

The operations manager's approval question is one discovery lead. That person might be a user or purchase influencer; purchasing authority and urgency are unknown. A candidate product segment is teams whose operations managers repeatedly coordinate approvals, with the buyer still to identify.

Prepare a short discovery conversation about their most recent approval cycle: frequency, delays, consequences, current tool, and who can approve a change. Then seek similar independent cases and a concrete trial commitment if the current product fits. No contact or market commitment follows from the supplied evidence alone.
